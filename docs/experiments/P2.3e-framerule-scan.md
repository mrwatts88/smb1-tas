**Session 17, 2026-08-24.** Opened after the user challenged the campaign's allocation, and was right.

## 0. Why this unit exists — the allocation argument, in this project's own numbers

Two lines from `STATUS.md` "Key numbers", which have been sitting there since P0:

> **Per-level slack/deficit (frames):** 1-1 20/**1** (closed end to end), 1-2 13/**8** (5 on Maru's route, S),
> 4-1 12/**9**, 4-2 8/**13**, 8-1 3/18, 8-2 2/19, 8-3 11/**10** (FPG+242: 7, S), 8-4 unquantized
>
> **WR vs MrWint segment optima:** gap 0 on all 10 solved segments (1-1 x4, **1-2 opening**, 8-4 x5)

And F52, recorded in P0.8 and never acted on:

> Prior exhaustive-search tool: MrWint/smb-opt is a **player-physics-only** model (enemies ignored) used for
> **segment** searches from **hand-picked states**. **No whole-level or full-state search exists anywhere.**
> Best-known 1-2 (**Maru370 2024**) is **5 frames** from the next framerule.

Put together:

1. **The WR is not optimal, and that is already documented.** Maru370's 2024 1-2 is **3 frames faster than
   HappyLee's** (H22/F52). The 2011 WR is beatable on a level someone already beat it on.
2. **The levels we have spent the campaign on are the two that were already solved.** 8-4 is where MrWint
   solved five segments; 4-2 we closed ourselves. Meanwhile **4-1, 8-3, 8-1, 8-2 and all of 1-2 past its
   71-frame opening have never been exhaustively searched by anyone.**
3. **Our tooling is the thing F52 says does not exist** — whole-level, full-state, enemy-aware search with
   admissible bounds and core-verified replays. It was pointed at the pre-solved levels.
4. **Calibration:** the one time it was pointed at an unoptimised route (4-2's top route) it found **35
   frames** of movement (553 vs 588). That died only because 4-2's wrong warp charges a ~31-frame key tax
   (F123). **1-2, 4-1 and 8-3 have no such tax** — a frame found is a frame banked against the deficit.

**Order: 1-2 first** (cheapest deficit at 5, and unsearched), then 8-3 (7 with FPG+242), then 4-1 (9).

# Part 2 — the `W12Warp` case is built and tracks the WR for 919 of 1280 frames

## 3. What shipped this session

**`BB12W` — 1-2's map including the warp zone (200 columns).** MrWint's `BB12` stops at 192, eight columns
short of the warp pipes. Built as `BB12` (cols 0..191) **plus** the dump's cols 192..199, because the
reconstruction can only read columns the parser had not already passed — it read 16..191 and agreed with
`BB12` on **every one of those 176 columns**, so the merge is `BB12` + the tail, not a re-derivation. (First
attempt used the raw reconstruction and Mario fell through the floor at x 40: cols 0..15 were never read and
came out as zeros. Worth remembering — the tool's "0 differing cells over the **176 read columns**" is a
statement about what it read, not about the whole map.)

**`W12Warp`** (`case/w12.rs`): control -> the world-4 warp pipe, `Small`, `WithRunningTimer`, `WithScrollPos`,
`WithBlockStates`, `EnterVerticalPipe<U178, U8>`, goal gated on `screen_left_abs >= 2816`, bound =
`build_overshoot_bound(2928, 2860)`.

**Start frame: fceux row 2486, not the GES-7 control frame at 2469.** The GES 7 -> 8 handoff **duplicates a
frame** — rows 2485 and 2486 are byte-identical — so a case rooted at 2469 runs one frame ahead of the core
from there on. Mario is mid-fall at 2486, so the start carries `y_pos 0x13068` and `y_spd 0x258`. Span =
3766 - 2486 = **1280 frames**. (`iterate_entrance` is wrong here: 1-2's entrance animation is not 4-2's and
iterating it lands 5 frames into the fall.)

**Block states:** 1-2 has **496 plain bricks** (bounce only) and exactly **11** cells that change metatile —
the five question blocks of the opening row at (10..14, 7) plus six item bricks — so the 16-bit `used` mask
fits with room to spare.

**Enemy hook `w12_enemies`:** the same engine as 4-2 and 8-4, with 1-2's `E_UndergroundArea1` bytes, six
pipes enumerated per F136 (cols 103, 109, 115 and the three warp pipes 178/182/186), and `BB12W` terrain.
Start ext = the two goombas already walking in at x 247 / 263 plus the loader at `EnemyDataOffset` 4.

## 5. The next blocker, precisely

At fceux row 3405 Mario **lands on a moving platform**: the core carries two `$27` objects in slots 2 and 3,
both at x 2236, one descending through y 195 and one at y 35 — 1-2's **elevator pair**. `class_of_id` has no
`$27` class (4-2's single lift is a bespoke hook, `w42_lift_u16`, not a class), so it falls through to the
inert `CLASS_ITEM`: right for slot occupancy, wrong for motion, and Mario cannot stand on it.

So the port order for 1-2 is: **`$27` small platform (elevators) -> `$26` large platform -> `$03` red koopa**.
The `$34` `WarpZoneObject` needs no class — its effect is the case's `screen_left_abs >= 2816` goal
condition.

## 6. The number that makes this worth finishing

`build_overshoot_bound(2928, 2860)` reports **30,720 end classes with 7 distinct return costs, [39..45]**.
The WR's own return — from the frame the scroll arms the warp zone (row 3720, x 2944) to the pipe entry
(row 3766) — is **46 frames**. The cheapest end class returns in **39**.

That gap is 7 frames, against a deficit of **5** for the framerule (Maru's route) or 8 (the WR's). It is the
same shape as F133's 8-4 room-3 question — *is a cheap end class reachable at the right step?* — except that
here the answer has never been looked for, and the bound's floor is 7 below the WR rather than 1.

**Caveat, stated up front:** that 39 is an **x-only** return cost — it ignores the vertical part of the
turnaround, exactly the coupling that cost 14 frames of slack in 8-4 room 2 (F137). So it is an upper bound
on the prize, not a promise. The honest reading is "worth the port", not "7 frames are there".

# Part 3 — the case is exact on all 1280 frames, and two real engine bugs fell out of it

## 7. Finishing the port

| object | what it needed |
|---|---|
| `$27` / `$26` | **`CLASS_LIFTPLAT`** — `LargePlatformSubroutines` sends both to `MoveLargeLiftPlat`, so one class covers them. Motion is `YMF_Dummy += Y_MoveForce; Y_Position += Y_Speed + carry` (10982) with Y wrapping in the byte and `Enemy_Y_HighPos` untouched. Init: `PlatLiftDown` ($27) = force `$f0` / speed `$00`; `PlatLiftUp` ($26) = `$10` / `$ff`; both then `PosPlatform` with Y = 1, which adds **12 px to the X**, not the Y. Mario meets it through `LargePlatformCollision` + `PositionPlayerOnVPlat`, never `PlayerEnemyCollision` — the collision flag uses the platform's **pre-move** position and the placement its **post-move** one. **4-2's lift is the same object**, so its validated collision code was reused verbatim. |
| `$03` | **`CLASS_REDKOOPA`** — `InitRedKoopa` = `InitNormalEnemy` + `Enemy_State = 1`, and `ChkForRedKoopa` (12550) turns it around instead of walking off a ledge, then `rts`es (no state update, no side check). |
| `$34` | no class: its effect is the case's `screen_left_abs >= 2816` goal condition. |

## 8. Two engine bugs the new level exposed

**(a) `CheckpointEnemyID`'s +8 is for ids < `$15` only.** The loader places an object at `row * 16`; the
`adc #$08` and `EnemyOffscrBitsMasked = 1` sit *after* a `cmp #$15 / bcs InitEnemyRoutines`, and the ROM's
own comment says "($00-$14 only)". The model applied both unconditionally, which put 1-2's elevators **8 px
below** the core's. Platforms, powerups and vines are all affected.

**(b) Bounding boxes were never clamped at the screen edges — and this one was live in 4-2 and 8-4 too.**
Boxes are screen-**relative** bytes, so an object near an edge has a box that wraps. `CheckRightScreenBBox`
/ `CheckLeftScreenBBox` (12891) push such a box fully offscreen (`$ff` right, `$00` left) so it cannot
collide with something at the opposite edge. Without it, **two goombas 250 px apart turned each other
around** — 18 of 150 trials in the first 1-2 battery, every one of them a phantom stomp. Fixed for enemy
boxes and the player's.

**The bound is TIGHT for the last 46 frames** (step 1234 = x 2944, the overshoot apex where the scroll arms
the warp zone). An admissible bound that equals the true remaining is a proof: **no path from the WR's
step-1234 state reaches the pipe in fewer than 46 frames**, with no search at all.

Then the exhaustive rungs, rooted on the WR's own state (a goal would be a genuine improvement on it):

| rung | horizon | result |
|---|---|---|

**So the WR's last 60 frames of 1-2 cannot be improved by one frame.** The wall is terrain, not slack: at

1200 (x 2884) he is in open ground and it explodes.

## 11. Where this converges with 8-4

Both levels now stall for the *same* reason: an **x-only bound that cannot price a turnaround**. 8-4 room 2
loses 14 frames to "must land before jumping"; 1-2 loses ~22 to the vertical part of the warp-zone
overshoot. The **coupled end-game term** queued as `P2.2f-bound` is therefore not a one-level fix — it is
the single piece of machinery that would push both ladders deeper, and it is the highest-value engineering
item on the board.

## 12. The wall clip — where 1-2's bound slack actually goes, and a first-arrival-gate near-miss

The slack profile has one sharp feature. Slack is **55 from step 40 to ~500** and **54** until ~1080 — i.e.
the bound and the truth advance in lockstep for a thousand frames, because Mario is at the **speed cap
(x speed 40) for essentially the whole level**. Then between step 1080 and 1120 the slack collapses
**54 -> 23**: the WR spends 40 frames where the bound expects 9.

That stretch is 1-2's **wall clip**. At step ~1087 Mario runs into the exit-pipe column (`BB12W` cols
168/169 are pipe from row 0 to 6, cols 170..176 are floor-to-ceiling brick), his x speed drops 40 -> 0, and
his x then creeps 2677 -> 2700 over ~36 frames while the speed ramps back up. The model reproduces it
exactly — it is inside the 1280/1280.

### The near-miss, recorded because it is the trap this project keeps documenting

A position-goal probe from the WR's step-1080 state, `--goal-x 2700 --goal-y 64`, found a goal at layer
**33** where the WR needs **42**. Reconstructed and **core-verified: 33/33 frames, 0 mismatches**. Nine
frames, apparently, on an unsearched level.

It is worth nothing. The arrival state is `x 2700, Y 61, x speed ~0, JUMPING, moving LEFT, side-collision
timer 15` against the WR's `x 2700, Y 64, x speed 21, STANDING`. Continuing from it to the next milestone
(`x >= 2784 && Y <= 64`) is **pruned at layer 1** — the admissible bound alone proves it needs **>= 47**
frames, which is exactly what the WR pays from *its* step-1113 state. The nine frames are paid straight
back.

This is F115 / H39 again, at the finest possible granularity: **a first-arrival gate on a scalar is not a
seam.** The gate said "9 frames ahead"; the physics said "same cost, worse state". Every position-goal
result has to be continued before it means anything.

### What the same probe does establish

From the WR's own step-1113 state, that milestone is **dry at deadline 46** (exhaustive, frontier extinct
at layer 16) and **reached at 47** — and the WR pays 47. **So the WR is optimal on that stretch too.**

*(Correction worth recording: the first reading of this called it "47 vs the WR's 48" and therefore a frame.
That came from an off-by-one in the fceux-row -> model-step mapping — model step k is fceux row **2486 + k**,
so the WR's arrival is step 1160, 47 frames from 1113, not 48. Always take the reference costs from the
model's own `--check-path` step numbering, never from a hand-mapped RAM row.)*

floor comes out solid from column 0. `tools/blockmap_from_dump.py data/wr/fceux_wr.ram 3816 5424 --rust BB41`
is the command; the map is 232 columns and the staircase and flagpole are visible at cols ~200-226.
