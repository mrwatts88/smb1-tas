## 6. The number that makes this worth finishing

# Part 3 — the case is exact on all 1280 frames, and two real engine bugs fell out of it

## 7. Finishing the port

| object | what it needed |
|---|---|
| `$27` / `$26` | **`CLASS_LIFTPLAT`** — `LargePlatformSubroutines` sends both to `MoveLargeLiftPlat`, so one class covers them. Motion is `YMF_Dummy += Y_MoveForce; Y_Position += Y_Speed + carry` (10982) with Y wrapping in the byte and `Enemy_Y_HighPos` untouched. Init: `PlatLiftDown` ($27) = force `$f0` / speed `$00`; `PlatLiftUp` ($26) = `$10` / `$ff`; both then `PosPlatform` with Y = 1, which adds **12 px to the X**, not the Y. Mario meets it through `LargePlatformCollision` + `PositionPlayerOnVPlat`, never `PlayerEnemyCollision` — the collision flag uses the platform's **pre-move** position and the placement its **post-move** one. **4-2's lift is the same object**, so its validated collision code was reused verbatim. |
| `$03` | **`CLASS_REDKOOPA`** — `InitRedKoopa` = `InitNormalEnemy` + `Enemy_State = 1`, and `ChkForRedKoopa` (12550) turns it around instead of walking off a ledge, then `rts`es (no state update, no side check). |
| `$34` | no class: its effect is the case's `screen_left_abs >= 2816` goal condition. |

## 8. Two engine bugs the new level exposed

**(a) `CheckpointEnemyID`'s +8 is for ids < `$15` only.** The loader places an object at `row * 16`; the
`adc #$08` and `EnemyOffscrBitsMasked = 1` sit *after* a `cmp #$15 / bcs InitEnemyRoutines`, and the ROM's
own comment says "($00-$14 only)". The model applied both unconditionally, which put 1-2's elevators **8 px
below** the core's. Platforms, powerups and vines are all affected.

**(b) Bounding boxes were never clamped at the screen edges — and this one was live in 4-2 and 8-4 too.**
Boxes are screen-**relative** bytes, so an object near an edge has a box that wraps. `CheckRightScreenBBox`
/ `CheckLeftScreenBBox` (12891) push such a box fully offscreen (`$ff` right, `$00` left) so it cannot
collide with something at the opposite edge. Without it, **two goombas 250 px apart turned each other
around** — 18 of 150 trials in the first 1-2 battery, every one of them a phantom stomp. Fixed for enemy
boxes and the player's.

## 11. Where this converges with 8-4

Both levels now stall for the *same* reason: an **x-only bound that cannot price a turnaround**. 8-4 room 2
loses 14 frames to "must land before jumping"; 1-2 loses ~22 to the vertical part of the warp-zone
overshoot. The **coupled end-game term** queued as `P2.2f-bound` is therefore not a one-level fix — it is
the single piece of machinery that would push both ladders deeper, and it is the highest-value engineering
item on the board.

## 12. The wall clip — where 1-2's bound slack actually goes, and a first-arrival-gate near-miss

The slack profile has one sharp feature. Slack is **55 from step 40 to ~500** and **54** until ~1080 — i.e.
the bound and the truth advance in lockstep for a thousand frames, because Mario is at the **speed cap
(x speed 40) for essentially the whole level**. Then between step 1080 and 1120 the slack collapses
**54 -> 23**: the WR spends 40 frames where the bound expects 9.

That stretch is 1-2's **wall clip**. At step ~1087 Mario runs into the exit-pipe column (`BB12W` cols
168/169 are pipe from row 0 to 6, cols 170..176 are floor-to-ceiling brick), his x speed drops 40 -> 0, and
his x then creeps 2677 -> 2700 over ~36 frames while the speed ramps back up. The model reproduces it
exactly — it is inside the 1280/1280.

### The near-miss, recorded because it is the trap this project keeps documenting

A position-goal probe from the WR's step-1080 state, `--goal-x 2700 --goal-y 64`, found a goal at layer
**33** where the WR needs **42**. Reconstructed and **core-verified: 33/33 frames, 0 mismatches**. Nine
frames, apparently, on an unsearched level.

It is worth nothing. The arrival state is `x 2700, Y 61, x speed ~0, JUMPING, moving LEFT, side-collision
timer 15` against the WR's `x 2700, Y 64, x speed 21, STANDING`. Continuing from it to the next milestone
(`x >= 2784 && Y <= 64`) is **pruned at layer 1** — the admissible bound alone proves it needs **>= 47**
frames, which is exactly what the WR pays from *its* step-1113 state. The nine frames are paid straight
back.

This is F115 / H39 again, at the finest possible granularity: **a first-arrival gate on a scalar is not a
seam.** The gate said "9 frames ahead"; the physics said "same cost, worse state". Every position-goal
result has to be continued before it means anything.

### What the same probe does establish

From the WR's own step-1113 state, that milestone is **dry at deadline 46** (exhaustive, frontier extinct
at layer 16) and **reached at 47** — and the WR pays 47. **So the WR is optimal on that stretch too.**

*(Correction worth recording: the first reading of this called it "47 vs the WR's 48" and therefore a frame.
That came from an off-by-one in the fceux-row -> model-step mapping — model step k is fceux row **2486 + k**,
so the WR's arrival is step 1160, 47 frames from 1113, not 48. Always take the reference costs from the
model's own `--check-path` step numbering, never from a hand-mapped RAM row.)*

## 17. And they do not carry — because the warp zone arms on the SCROLL, not on Mario

Continuing from that state, the WR's own remaining inputs do not work verbatim (the trajectory drifts and
overshoots the entry window by 1 px), so the tail was re-searched:

1. From step 1157, reaching the WR's step-1220 milestone (`x >= 2933 && Y <= 128`) takes **exactly 60**
   frames — and `h(root) = 60 = the deadline`, so 60 is provably the minimum. That puts us at step **1217**,
   still **3 ahead**. Core-verified over the whole 137-frame segment: **0 mismatches**.
2. From step 1217, reaching the warp pipe in 60 frames — the cost the WR pays from *its* step-1220 state —
   is **DRY at layer 9**.

The three frames are refunded. The mechanism is structural and worth stating plainly:

> **`ScrollLockObject_Warp` arms `WarpZoneControl = 4` when SCREEN LEFT reaches its locked maximum 2816
> — a property of where Mario has BEEN, not where he is.**

At step 1217 we are at the same x as the WR is at 1220 (both x 2935) with far more speed (40 vs 22) and more
height (Y 107 vs 124) — but our ScreenLeft is **2806** against the WR's **2809**. Arriving three frames
early at the same place means the scroll is three pixels behind, and the scroll only advances when Mario
pushes it. **In 1-2, a frame banked before the warp zone is a frame lent to the scroll.**

## 18. The census WAS run, and the refund is not a pick artifact

Runbook §3.2 says never trust the auto-pick, and the binding quantity here is the scroll, not x speed — so
`smb-opt offset-census` gained a **`--sl`** flag that ranks a layer by **absolute ScreenLeft, descending**
instead of by scroll offset. (The two are different questions: offset is what 4-2's mint work cared about;

## 22. Pricing the two RNG blockers at code level — and 8-3's may not be one

F130's method applied to the two remaining candidates: do not assume randomness makes an object unmodellable,
read how the randomness actually enters.

**The LFSR itself is frame-indexed.** `PseudoRandomBitReg` is advanced at `PauseSkip` in the NMI's timer
block, immediately after `inc FrameCounter`, as an unconditional 7-byte rotate with feedback from d1 of
bytes 0 and 1 (smbdis.asm:802-815). Nothing about the player enters it. **On a lag-free route the whole
register is a pure function of the frame count and can simply be precomputed per step** — the same
conclusion F130 reached for the cheep frenzy's timer.

**The Hammer Bro (`$05`, 8-3) then prices out cheaply.** `ProcHammerBro` (9212) uses the register in exactly
three places:

| use | what it decides | cost |
|---|---|---|
| `SpawnHammerObj`: `PseudoRandomBitReg+1 & 7` (or `& 8`) | which misc slot the hammer goes in, hence whether the throw succeeds | frame-indexed -> **precomputable** |
| `HammerBroJumpCode`: `PseudoRandomBitReg+1,x & 1` | vertical speed `$fa` vs `$fd`, and **only** while the bro is below the middle of the screen | frame-indexed, 2-valued -> **precomputable** |
| `HammerBroJumpCode`: `$00 & PseudoRandomBitReg+2,x` -> jump-length offset | `HammerBroJumpLData` index | **inert in normal play**: the offset is only used when `SecondaryHardMode` is set (`bne HJump`; otherwise `tay` forces offset 0), so the jump length is always `$20` |

So the Hammer Bro is not a branching factor at all — it is a deterministic object whose decisions read a
frame-indexed register. **8-3's long-standing "the model has no RNG" blocker looks like it dissolves the
same way F126's did.** That has to be confirmed by a difftest, not by this argument alone, but it moves 8-3
from "blocked" to "port it and see".

**Lakitu (`$11`, 4-1) is the harder of the two** and stays unpriced here: it reads the same frame-indexed
register, but it also tracks Mario's position continuously, so the coupling is to the trajectory rather than
to the frame index alone — closer to the cheep-frenzy *spawn law* than to the Hammer Bro's timers.

**Queue consequence:** 8-3 leads 4-1 on all three axes — smaller deficit (7 vs 9), no new block map needed,
and the cheaper RNG object. It is the next level.

# Part 7 — the ordering was already right; the bug was a coin nobody touched, and then a scroll lock

## 28. §26's diagnosis was wrong, and the correction is worth stating plainly

§26 concluded that `run_step` updated the parser too early in the frame. It does not. The order is

```rust
let result = self.player_ctrl_routine();          // coin awards happen in here
if O::BlockStates::ON { self.block_states_game_engine(); }   // the parser, after the player
```

which is exactly `GameEngine`'s order — the player routines, then `UpdScrollVar` -> `RunParser` at the end.
A coin awarded on frame N already stalls frame N's parser in the model.

What was actually happening at step 446 is that **the model never awarded that coin**. Mario is rising
through column 68 and his head check hits the **lower** cell first: `BB12W[7][68] = $52`, a plain brick. The
`$c2` at `(68, 6)` is the coin sitting **on top of** the brick, and in SMB1 bumping a block takes the coin
above it:

```asm
BumpBlock:  jsr CheckTopOfBlock     ; and BrickShatter does the same
CheckTopOfBlock:
       ldy $02 / beq TopEx          ; row 0 has nothing above it
       ... sbc #$10 ...             ; one row up in the block buffer
       lda ($06),y / cmp #$c2       ; is it a coin?
       lda #$00 / sta ($06),y       ; erase it
       jsr RemoveCoin_Axe           ; -> VRAM_Buffer_AddrCtrl = 6  -> the parser stalls
       jsr SetupJumpCoin            ; -> GiveOneCoin -> CoinTally++
```

Confirmed at both of the WR's two hits, straight out of the core's RAM:

| frame | block object | cell | `CoinTally` | `AddrCtrl` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| row 2887 (step 399) | slot 1 spawns, abs X 960, Y 142 | **(60, 7)**, coin at (60, 6) | 19 -> 20 | 6 |
| row 2934 (step 446) | slot 0 spawns, abs X 1088, Y 142 | **(68, 7)**, coin at (68, 6) | 20 -> 21 | 6 |

`SetupJumpCoin` has exactly **one** caller in the ROM, so `CheckTopOfBlock` is the only bump path that can
stall the parser. The other bump-awards-a-coin route, `CoinBlock` (a `$c0`/`$5f`/`$58`/`$5d` whose
*contents* is a coin, reached through `BumpBlock`'s `BlockBumpedChk` jump table), calls `GiveOneCoin`
**without** `RemoveCoin_Axe` — status-bar digits only, no `AddrCtrl 6`, no stall. So the model is right to
ignore it, and 4-2's `$c0` at column 51 (the one coin its model misses) costs nothing. That is F149.

## 29. And then the parser control found a second, larger bug: `ScrollLock` (F150)

With `CheckTopOfBlock` in, the parser matched for 1081 steps and then went one frame early on every
remaining burst. The cause was not the parser at all — the model's **screen-left was 2 px ahead of the
core** from step 1058 to the end of the level, and the burst trigger is `(sl >> 5)` changing.

1-2's warp zone is built out of three objects the model had none of:

| object | where | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| `ScrollLockObject` (row 13) | `L_UndergroundArea1` +148, page 11 col 2 = **column 178** | `AreaParserCore` on that column **toggles** `ScrollLock`; `ScrollHandler` opens with `lda ScrollLock / bne InitScrlAmt`, so a set flag means **no scroll at all** that frame |
| `WarpZoneObject` (`$34`) | `E_UndergroundArea1` +42, page 11 col 2 | idles until `ScrollLock` is set, then clears it, `inc WarpZoneControl`, erases itself — **only** on a frame where `Player_Y_Position AND Player_Y_HighPos` is 0, i.e. (underground) **Mario's Y is even** |
| `ScrollLockObject_Warp` (row 13) | `L_UndergroundArea1` +160, page 12 col 6 = **column 198** | sets `WarpZoneControl` (4 for world 1), kills every piranha plant, and **falls straight through into `ScrollLockObject`** — there is no `rts` between them — so it locks the scroll for the rest of the level |

On the WR: lock on at frame 3544, cleared at 3545 (Mario's Y was even), **2 px gone for good**; then armed
and relocked at 3720 with `ScreenLeft` at its final 2816. The model now reproduces all three transitions on

Two things follow for the search:

- The goal was `ScreenLeft >= 2816`. That is *necessary* but **optimistic** — arming needs the parser to

- **Holding the lock is a real cost that a searched path can pay by accident**: if Mario's Y is odd on the
  frame after column 178 is parsed, the screen simply does not scroll, and it keeps not scrolling until his

## 33. The residue, run as one piece: a joint two-segment bucketed beam

§21 named the one experiment that could still bank those three frames: *"a search that **optimises the
scroll** through the clip — rather than reaching a position gate and checking the scroll afterwards."*
Chaining clip -> carry -> final cannot do that, because the clip's arrival is chosen by a position goal and
then whatever scroll it happens to have is what the rest inherits. The fix is to delete the intermediate
goal entirely and search **steps 1080 -> the pipe in one piece**, letting the search trade position for
scroll however it likes:

```
bfscx W12Warp data/wr/wr_inputs.bin 2486 1080 199 --enemies 0 \
  --beam 5000 --beam-buckets x,off,y,spd,e --beam-max 3000000 --check-path 200
```

- **Deadline 199 against the WR's 200**, so *any* goal is at least one frame. The goal is the case's own —
  `EnterVerticalPipe(178, 8)` **and** `blocks.warp_armed` — which is the exact game condition (F150).
- Root bound **146** against deadline 199: **53 frames of slack**, far past exhaustive. Hence the beam.
- Buckets `x,off,y,spd,e` per runbook §7.3's *discovery* rule plus F138's absolute-x band and H40's enemy
  axis. `off` is in there because the scroll is the binding quantity here (§18).
- `--check-path 200` piggybacked: **0 bound violations over the WR's own 200 steps**, and the per-layer
  report says whether the WR is still in the frontier — the diagnosis that F138 needed.

**A first sizing attempt was wrong and is recorded as such:** `--beam 8` with buckets `x,off,e` realised
only 1-2 buckets in the early layers, so it was an 8-wide beam and the frontier went extinct at layer 52.
Widened to 5000 per bucket over five dimensions it behaves — 167 buckets and 488k states by layer 20.

Per runbook §7.2 this is a **discovery** run: a goal it finds is a candidate to core-verify, and a goal it
misses proves nothing.
